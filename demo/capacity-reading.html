How many brokers does this workload actually need?

Vertical scaling on Solace Cloud has a ceiling. When message sizes or adoption keep growing, the question stops being "which bigger service class" and becomes "how many brokers, and when." This is that decision, worked three ways against a real compiled capacity model — with the reasoning shown, not hidden behind a number.

the thesis

One broker's ceiling collapses as messages get bigger

A single enterprise-10k broker sustains ~44,000 guaranteed 1 KB messages per second — but only ~439 per second at 200 KB. The curve is why "just add brokers" is sometimes the wrong answer: past a point you are byte-bound, and the cheaper fix is a smaller payload.

Throughput falls ~100× while byte-rate barely doubles (45 → 90 MB/s). The broker is moving bytes, not messages — so the binding constraint flips to bytes, and adding brokers scales linearly against a cost that large payloads make brutal.

the business case

Broker counts become dollars, and dollars get projected

A recommendation an architect can defend needs a cost and a forward look. With an operator's own price table the report turns counts into monthly spend; the what-if re-runs the same pure engine under load multipliers to show where the fleet ceiling actually bites.
